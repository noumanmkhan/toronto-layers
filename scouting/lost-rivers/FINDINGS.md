# Lost rivers: what open data has (October 11, 2026)

Rule agreed with the user: geometry only from openly licensed data; a river with no open course is left
out rather than traced from a map. Scout: `scout.py` (Overpass, OpenStreetMap ODbL), output in `out/`.

## Toronto
- **Not in OpenStreetMap at all:** Garrison Creek, Taddle Creek, Russell Creek, Castle Frank's buried
  lower course, Brewery Creek, Chaplin Creek. The best-known lost rivers have no open geometry.
- **No open dataset either:** City of Toronto Open Data has no historical or buried watercourse layer
  (CKAN searches for watercourse/creek/buried return nothing). Published lost-river maps (Lost Rivers
  Toronto, books, blogs) are copyrighted.
- **What OSM does map inside the city** is short culverts on creeks that are still mostly open, not the
  historic buried courses: Burke Brook 1.6 of 5.0 km in a culvert, Yellow Creek 1.1 of 3.1, Mud Creek
  0.7 of 2.1, Lavender Creek 0.4 of 1.7, plus fully piped short reaches in the suburbs (Dorset, Adam's,
  Centennial, Malvern, Wilson, Vyner, Newtonbrook, Albion, Westminster creeks, each under 1.1 km).

## Chicago
- No lost waterway is mapped inside the city: no Mud Lake (the portage), no old South Branch channel
  (straightened 1928-30), no filled slips. Inside the city OSM has only today's rivers and canals
  (North and South Branch, Bubbly Creek as the South Fork, the Calumet rivers, Ogden Slip).
- A Chicago version would be stories on today's waterways (the river reversed in 1900, Bubbly Creek and
  the stockyards), not lost courses.

## Options for the user
1. **Story points, no lines:** a marker per lost river at places tied to it in public sources (Garrison
   Creek: Christie Pits, Bickford Park, Trinity Bellwoods' ravine; Taddle Creek: Philosopher's Walk, Wychwood
   Park; Russell Creek: Russell Hill Road...), each with a short story and a card row near them. No traced
   course, so the rule holds.
2. **Approximate courses:** hand-drawn from public descriptions (street names), labelled approximate.
   The user chose against this on October 11.
3. **Park it** until someone maps the courses in OpenStreetMap (anyone can; then the layer just reads them).
